The spread (or "line") is the standard way the market expresses how balanced a game is considered. If a team is "favored by 4.5 points," it means they're expected to win by more than 4.5 points to be considered the winner from that line's perspective.

The spread isn't a prediction of the final score — it's a tool for balancing interest on both sides, set by sportsbooks based on statistical analysis and where money is being wagered. That's why it shifts constantly; for example, a significant injury announced a few days before a game can move the line by several points.

For a reader new to American football, the simplest way to understand the spread is as a quick indicator of how close a game is considered by experts: the bigger the line, the clearer the gap in level between the two teams is thought to be.
